# Tools and Weapons

Pickaxes, axes, swords, bows, shields and the rest are not a fixed list of wooden, stone, iron, golden, diamond and netherite kinds. Every one is **assembled from parts**, and what it does comes from what those parts are made of. Vanilla's tool recipes do not exist (a wooden or stone pickaxe, axe and shovel still craft by hand, so you can build the first table, and are converted like any old tool, below); make the parts at a bench (the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md), [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md) or [Stonecutter](Stonecutter.md)) and put them together in a crafting grid.

Armor works the same way: see [Armor](Armor.md).

## Making the parts

A pickaxe is a **head** on a **handle**, and the head is a part of its own. Shape it first at the bench for its stock: the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) (metal, with a [Hammer](Hammer.md)), the [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md) (wood, bone, leather, fiber, with an axe) or the [Stonecutter](Stonecutter.md) (stone, ceramic, glass, crystal, with a [Sawblade](Sawblade.md)). Put stock in the cells first; the list then shows only the parts that stock can make. Fill the cells (the **cells** column below); the hammer, axe or saw loses a point. What comes out is one `Pickaxe Head`, whatever it's made of; the material rides on the item.

| Part | Cells (the shape is only a picture) |
| --- | --- |
| **Pickaxe head** | `XXX` |
| **Axe head** | `XX / X_` |
| **Shovel head** | `X_X / _X_` |
| **Hoe head** | `XX` |
| **Sword blade** | `X / X` |
| **Pickaxe head, heavy** | `XXX / _X_` | four ingots: a heavier head that mines a three-by-three |
| **Axe head, bearded** | `XXX / X__` | four ingots: a heavier head that hits a little harder |
| **Cleaver head** | `XX / XX` |
| **Pike head** | `X / X / X` |
| **Maul head** | `XXX / XXX` |
| **Sickle head** | `XX_ / __X` |
| **Trident tines** | `_X_ / X_X` |
| **Tip** | one cell: a gem, shard or nugget |
| **Binding** | one cell: leather, string or wire. Polymer strings (Polypropylene, Polyethylene, PVC, PTFE, Synthetic Rubber, Bitumen and Soul Resin String) bind too |
| **Sword guard**, **sword pommel**, **shield rim** | one cell each |
| **Crossbow prod** | two cells: sticks, rods or bone |

Every piece must be the same stock. **Stock** is ingots, gems, planks, cobblestone or bone (tines take ingots, gems or shards). Anything of a material that fits works, so a head can be iron, bronze, gold, diamond, stone, a plank or a bone.

**Steel and other hot-worked metals have to be hot to shape**, exactly as under a [hammer](Hammer.md); the part comes out as hot as the stock was. Metals without a hot-working window (iron, wood, stone) shape cold.

## Putting one together

Put one part in the center of a bench (the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md), [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md) or [Stonecutter](Stonecutter.md); guns at the [Gun Table](Gun_Table.md)) and fit the others to it, in any order: the bench's fan shows what you are carrying that attaches. Each shaped part is **one item**; **handles** are sticks, rods or bone, stacked as many as the handle is long; all of one part must be the same material. The table works out what you are making from the parts.

| Item | Parts |
| --- | --- |
| **Pickaxe** | head, two handles, binding |
| **Axe** | head, two handles, binding |
| **Shovel** | head, two handles, binding |
| **Hoe** | head, two handles, binding |
| **Sword** | blade, guard, grip |
| **Mace** | a [heavy core](Heavy_Core.md) (or a block of metal), two rods, binding |
| **Trident** | tines, two shafts, binding |
| **Bow** | three limbs, three lengths of string |
| **Crossbow** | a prod (two cells), string, three stock, a lock, **plus a Crossbow Blueprint** (below) |
| **Shield** | six faces, a rim part (a grip makes it five faces) |
| **Shears** | two blades (a pivot is optional) |
| **Twin-headed tool** | two heads of any kind (pick, axe, shovel, hoe), each its own material, one handle, binding: digs what both heads dig. A pick and an axe make a **mattock**, two axes a double-bit axe. A second head slows the swing and weakens the blow, because the handle carries both |
| **Cleaver** | head, grip, binding: a short, heavy axe that bites deeper than a full one |
| **Pike** | head, three handles, binding: reaches further |
| **Maul** | head, two handles, binding: splits wood and staggers what it hits |
| **Sickle** | head, grip, binding: cutting a crop or a leaf takes its neighbors too |

You can add:

- Every headed tool needs a **binding** (leather, string or wire) where head meets handle, and a sword needs a **guard** instead; nothing assembles without one. A sword, a pickaxe or a twin tool may add a **pommel**, a weight behind the hand: the knob is light, the ball heavier and the counterweight heaviest. A heavier pommel behind the hand brings the head round faster.
- A **tip** part, below.
- Armor and shields can take a **grip** or **lining**.

A tip, binding, guard, pommel, rim and crossbow prod are shaped at a bench first, like a head, so a stack of ingots is never ambiguous: the item says what it is.

### Tips

A pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, twin-headed tool, cleaver, sickle, pike or sword can have a **tip**: shape one gem, shard or nugget into a **Tip** at a bench, then fit it with the other parts (or add it to a finished tool in the center) and the tool is tipped. **The tip decides hardness**, so what the edge can dig is the tip's. It bites like the two together, and the body keeps the toughness and most of the wear. A steel pick with a diamond tip digs what diamond digs, chips like steel, and lasts about 1.6× as long as plain steel. A tip no harder than its body does nothing (a gold tip on iron is a pad). **Maces, mauls, tridents, bows, crossbows, shields and shears take no tip.** A tool made without a tip can be given one later at a bench; swapping in a different gem replaces it.

### Brittle heads

A whole diamond head is possible and bites superbly, but diamond is brittle: it keeps only a sixteenth of the durability its hardness suggests. Anything tougher than a fifth of its hardness (iron, stone, steel) is unaffected. Set diamond as a tip instead.

### Crossbow Blueprint

The crossbow is the one tool with a mechanism, so it needs a **Crossbow Blueprint** in the blueprint slot with the parts, made at the [Blueprint Table](Blueprint_Table.md). The blueprint must show the same parts as the ones put in, role for role (same material, same part, same count); ground-in qualities need not match, and a blueprint of a different design does not work.

## What the parts do

You won't see a stat sheet. Hold the tool and read the words on its tooltip: *head-heavy*, *light in the hand*, *heavy*, *keen*, *blunt*, *brittle*, *soft*, *loose-jointed*, *worn*. A tool is a physical object, and these are the things you'd feel picking it up. Below the words, the tooltip lists every part except the head (or blade) as a line such as "contains Leather Binding (1)", with the count at the end: a pickaxe's handle reads "(2)".

- **A heavier head hits harder and swings slower.** Gold is nearly three times iron's weight; a gold pickaxe is slow and hits hard, but gold is soft.
- **A longer handle reaches further** and puts more strain on the joint.
- **Hardness decides what it can dig.** An edge needs to be at least **3** hardness for iron ore and the stone class, **6** for gold, diamond and redstone ore, and **40** for obsidian and ancient debris. So copper and gold can't mine iron ore, iron can't mine obsidian, and diamond and netherite can. (Stone has a very high number, so a stone pickaxe *can* mine diamond ore. It chips doing it.)
- **How long it lasts** comes from how hard and strong the edge is. Iron lasts about as long as vanilla iron; gold barely lasts; a whole diamond head bites superbly but is [brittle](#brittle-heads), so diamond is better as a tip.
- **Enchanting** is done to a **part**, not the finished tool, at the [Enchanting Table](Enchanting_Table.md). How well a part takes one depends on its material: gold is best, stone is worst.

### Wearing it out the wrong way

A tool wears with use, like any hand tool, but misuse costs extra and tells you which kind:

- **An edge on something much harder than it can take chips.** A diamond pickaxe on obsidian wears quickly, because diamond is hard but brittle. A **keen** edge chips sooner; a **blunt** one is tougher.
- **A head too heavy for its handle works the joint loose.** Netherite or gold on a wooden handle gets *loose-jointed* and loses a bit more each swing. A stronger handle (bone, a rod) or a binding fixes it.
- **A bow's string is the limit.** Stiff limbs on a weak string shoot faster and draw slower, until the string is the weak point. Use wire for a string.

## Edge

An edged tool has an edge: **keen**, **standard** or **blunt**. Keen bites harder and chips sooner. Blunt is tougher.

A new tool comes with the standard edge unless a [Blueprint](Blueprint_Table.md) says otherwise. **Regrind** it at a **grindstone**: sneak and right-click the grindstone with the tool in hand to step to the next edge. Each grind costs **2 points of durability** (never the last), so an edge is a choice, not a toggle.

## When it breaks, mending, and swapping parts

A tool doesn't vanish when it's spent. It stops with one point left and acts like an empty hand until you repair it. It also gets visibly worse on the way down: *Worn*, *Battered*, *Nearly spent*.

**Repairing:** put the tool in the center of a bench, click its wearing part and pick stock of the material it is made of (an iron pickaxe with iron ingots). Each ingot puts back its share.

**Swapping a part:** put the tool in the center, click the part and pick a *different* part, or stock of a different material (a bronze pickaxe head on an iron pickaxe replaces its head; the old head comes back to you). A part can also be taken off, which leaves the part it was built on with the rest fitted to it. A new wearing part is fresh. Enchantments stay.

## Old vanilla tools

Vanilla tools, weapons and armor that turn up (in chests, on mobs, or in your pockets) are converted on the spot into the modular item they would have been, with the same name, enchantments and wear. An iron pickaxe is an iron head on a wooden handle with a leather binding.

**Diamond is the exception.** A diamond tool becomes a **steel** one with a **diamond tip** ("Diamond-tipped Steel Pickaxe"); diamond armor becomes steel with four diamond **studs**. A whole diamond head would be brittle and poor. The converted pick lasts about half as long as vanilla's diamond did and, as in vanilla, netherite remains the best tool in the game.

## Plating

A tool or armor piece whose main part is metal can be plated three ways: hung in an [Electrolytic Cell](Electrolytic_Cell.md#electroplating), dipped in a molten zinc, tin or glass bath ([Casting](Casting.md#hot-dipping)), or made from a plate clad at the [Rolling Mill](Rolling_Mill.md). A good coat adds the coat metal's corrosion resistance (a tool used wet rusts by how little its metal resists water). On **armor** a harder coat also hardens the plate a little. On a **tool** the edge is ground bare, so a coat protects the flats and changes neither bite nor hardness. Re-forging the main part removes the coat.

## Measuring

The tooltip gives words for free. To see a design's numbers *before* you make it, draw it at the [Blueprint Table](Blueprint_Table.md). For numbers on a finished piece, carry a [Balance Scale](Instruments.md#balance-scale) (mass) or a [Scratch File Set](Instruments.md#scratch-file-set) (hardness). The [Impact Pendulum](Instruments.md#impact-pendulum) reports edge toughness and the [Strike Post](Instruments.md#strike-post) reports the joules and damage of a blow.

### Forging variants by rhythm

Some variants are not laid out in cells but **forged**, at the [Anvil](Anvil.md#forging): put the stock in the center (the same cells as the standard shape, and hot if it is a hot-worked metal) and click the variant in the fan. Play the pattern around the stock: **tap** on the left lane (left click or Z) and **heavy** on the right (right click or X) as notes fall to a ring. A **metronome** clicks the tempo and counts four beats in before the first note. The window is generous, and each blow flashes Clean, Loose or Miss. A harder metal plays slower.

The stock cools as you play. Notes struck while it is too cold (or still too hot) are dead and count against you. A sloppy run never ruins the part, but it leaves **flaws**: each one takes a share of the edge's toughness and durability (a ruined part starts at half), and the part reads "rough", "flawed" or "ruined" on its tooltip. A clean run leaves none, though even a good player is sometimes a hair off.

**What you can forge:** a sword blade is forged **curved**, **tapered**, **serrated**, **fullered** or **leaf**-shaped; the plain blade is straight. A blade, a head or a limb also has **axes**: length, width and thickness of a blade, head size and edge length of a head, face size of a shield, limb length of a bow. Aim them at the bench before you make the part (see [Armor](Armor.md#axes-aiming-and-names) for how aiming works). More length reaches further, a wider blade hits harder, a thicker one resists chipping and a thinner one bites, and all of it is more stock and more weight. The range an axis sits in is a **band**, and a band can carry a signature: a **short** blade is a stabber (strike from behind), a **long** one a chopper (a heavy overhead blow), a **small** axe head is thrown, a **large** one fells trees, a large pickaxe head quarries, a large maul slams.

### Names

A finished tool or weapon is named by rule from its blade, head, face or limb: its shape and the bands its axes sit in. A band is a range, written here as "up to" its top figure; the last band of an axis is "over" the one before. A name gives no bonus: the perks come only from the parts, the bands and the shapes. A build that fits no row is named by its size, shape and material: size words for the bands that are not normal, then the shape and material, then the type, as in "Wide Serrated Iron Sword". A name never repeats a word its rule already needs: a Dagger is not a "Short Dagger". A hard tip or stud set into the part goes in front ("Iron-tipped Steel Sword").

### How each one fights

- **Pike.** Left click is a jab: long reach, but it cannot hit anything closer than 2 blocks. Hold right click to lower it into a charge: you keep sprinting while it is held out, and it tires and lowers as the charge runs out. While you and a target close at 4.6 blocks a second or faster, each thing in front of you at 2 to 4.5 blocks is struck once for damage that grows with your closing speed, the pike's strike and the weight of its head. From 5.1 blocks a second it knocks them back, and early in the charge it pulls riders off their mounts. The charge tires in three stages (full, then no dismount, then damage only), and a weakly bound pike tires sooner. A fast hit wears the pike more. A pike cannot crit, and Strength and Weakness do not change a charge.
- **Tapered blade.** Keep hitting the same target without pause and each hit hurts more; getting hit or switching target resets it.
- **Curved blade.** Wait before the first cut to land a heavy bleeding strike.
- **Serrated blade.** Great against the unarmored and for clearing brush, poor against armor.
- **Long blade.** Wait for a full swing and it hits everything around your target; mash it and it is weak.
- **Short blade.** Strike from behind or at unaware mobs, especially while sneaking; face to face it is worse than a plain blade.
- **Leaf blade.** A kill readies your next swing and speeds you up, and fighting a crowd hits harder.
- **Prospector's pick.** Right click to ping: ore within 12 blocks glows through the stone for 6 seconds. It is slow to recharge (10 seconds) and wears the pick, so ping and then dig.
- **Miner's pick.** Mine one ore and the connected ore of the same kind comes with it, up to 8 more, at a small extra wear each. It will not spend the pick's last point. Sneak to mine a single block.
- **Large axe head (a felling axe).** Break a log and the whole connected tree comes down, up to 24 logs. Each log costs extra durability, and sneaking breaks one log only.
- **Small axe head (a hatchet).** Hold right click to wind up, release to throw. It hits harder the farther it flies (up to 2.5 times), costs 2 durability per throw and lands as an item to pick up.
- **Bearded axe.** Hitting a raised shield disables it for 8 seconds and pulls the target toward you. Against a target with no shield it deals 10% less damage.

### Fighting with them

Melee weapons hit everything in their swept volume, can [parry](Combat.md#parry), and can be swung from the off hand. See [Combat](Combat.md). What a part is made of can also change what a blow does (silver against the undead, a blaze rod setting things alight): see [Material Perks](Material_Perks.md). Haste and Mining Fatigue change your swing cadence as they do in vanilla, and also scale the mod's own timers (a shield bash's cooldown, a hoe's recovery, a [gun's](Guns.md#handling) cycle and reload).

### Reading what sets a tool apart

Every modular tool names what sets it apart on one line under its parts. They come from three sources. Words the hand can feel come from the figures: head-heavy, light in the hand, heavy, light, keen, blunt, brittle, soft, loose-jointed. The trait comes from the part's shape or the tool's type: Flurry, Opening cut, Hack, Overhead, Quarry, Backstab, Flourish, Timber, Throw, Hook, Sounding, Vein, Charge. The shape is the variant the main part was made in, such as tapered or curved. **Hold Shift to read each in full:** a word says which figure decides it, where the line is and which part it comes from; a trait says what it does and what it costs; a shape says how it differs from the standard part. The bench shows a part's trait when you hover it in the list, before you make it.

Messages like the Flurry count, a forged part's quality and a Blueprint Table's print status appear as small cards at the bottom right, above your ammo counter if you hold a gun. They fade after a few seconds, and at most three show at once.

### The Suits line

Every gun, bow, crossbow, melee weapon and plated glove carries an aqua line on its tooltip, "Suits:", listing what it is good at: *heavy-hitting*, *quick*, *precise*, *quiet*. A word appears when the weapon scores 60% or more on that axis. All weapon kinds share one scale, so a slug gun and a maul can both be heavy-hitting.

- **Heavy-hitting**: the joules a hit carries, on a scale from 20 to 1500 J.
- **Quick**: hits a second, from half a hit to four.
- **Precise**: a gun's tight cone, a bow's flat flight, or a light, long, hard-edged blade. Bare hands score low, claws higher.
- **Quiet**: a gun's low report and light weight, a light blade with a dull edge, or a light plate.

Hold Shift to read each word with its percentage.

### Other tools, other shapes

Every shaped part comes in several shapes, laid in a different layout or forged to a different rhythm. Each is a way to play, with its price.

- **Pickaxes.** A heavy pick mines a three-by-three square of the face you strike, at a point of extra wear for each extra block. Sneak to mine one.
- **Shovels, hoes and sickles.** A scoop shovel digs a row three wide and a trench shovel digs a block deeper, both at extra wear. A kama tills a row of three, with a short wait afterwards. A rake pulls a harvest's drops to your feet. A scythe cuts a five-by-five of crops and leaves but hits mobs weakly. A billhook leaves a bleed on whatever it hits but its own blow is weaker. A reaping hook replants what it cuts from the seeds it drops, one plant at a time. Sneak to use any of them as the ordinary tool.
- **Heavy and thrust weapons.** A sledge maul smashes the ground and shoves everything around its target when fully charged, but a half-swing is weak. A war hammer is small but hits through armor and dents it, slowing the wearer. A butcher's cleaver hits animals hard and often gives a second helping of meat. A bardiche cuts through the first target into the one behind. A spear lunges hard at a sprint but is weak standing still. A harpoon, thrown, drags what it hits back toward you, and harder in water. A spring hammer (a maul head shaped 4x2 like the sledge, from any maul-head stock) is cocked with a right-click; the next blow adds **6 damage**, then the head needs **1.5 seconds** to reset. A spring blade (a single-piece spear head) is thrown from a spring at **160%** speed with almost no wind-up, and does not return. A pitchfork hits a small crowd for less each. A spring takes the reaction of what it hits, more against armored targets, and is rated by the head's edge toughness. Worked past its rating it takes a permanent set and stores less each time (a spring blade with a set throws less hard); far past it, it snaps, ruining a quarter of the tool's durability and hurting your hand for 2. A heavy spring blade head overloads its spring the same way. The mace has only one shape.
- **Armor.** Light shells use fewer ingots, weigh less and cover less; heavy ones use more, weigh more and slow you. Helmets: an open helm leaves you open to arrows, a great helm blunts them but makes mobs notice you from further away, a sallet softens blows from behind. Chestplates: a brigandine bites back in proportion to its studs, a breastplate takes the edge off falls and blasts, a hauberk stops blades but not maces. Leggings: greaves tear you through cobweb, powder snow and berry bushes (a share of the slow, by your mass), cuisses plant you against knockback at the cost of slower swimming. Boots: sabatons land on everything around your feet with their weight added to yours, by mass ratio, and make soft landings free, riding boots give a speed burst when you start to run. Only one piece of any given effect counts however many you wear. In the inventory, a piece of armor is drawn as the vanilla iron armor sprite, tinted by its plate material.
- **Bows, crossbows and shields.** A shortbow draws fast and shoots light. A longbow shoots hard and fast but takes longer to draw and slows you while drawn. A recurve is forged: a full draw hits harder and passes through a mob, a part draw does not. A hunting bow is forged and silent, and an arrow to an animal's head hits much harder. An arbalest hits hard, pierces two mobs, loads slowly and slows you while loading. A pistol crossbow loads very fast but is short-range and weak. A repeater loads three bolts at once and fires them scattered. A buckler is small and, on the [parry key](Combat.md#shields), turns a melee blow whole and throws the attacker back, but guards a narrow arc. A kite shield returns arrows and bolts the way you look when you press the parry key and is weaker against melee. A tower shield guards every angle and holds a lot, but slows you, wears fast, and using it while sprinting bashes mobs away.

A shield is drawn as a vanilla-style shield sized by its face: a buckler is small, a kite shield tall, a tower shield large. The face and grip are tinted by their materials, and a frame in the rim's material runs round the face, thicker for a flanged rim. Raising it shows the vanilla guard pose.

**Shields get hot.** Every blow a shield stops leaves heat in its face, more for a light face than a heavy one, and the heat stacks until the shield has rested (it cools by itself, even in a chest). Fire and lava warm the face towards their own temperature and no further. A hot face is felt: a shield too hot to hold hurts the hand, unless its grip or its face is wood, leather or another thing that does not carry heat, or you wear gloves. Near the temperature its material softens at, the shield holds less of each blow, and past it every blow wears it twice as hard. It never breaks all at once. A metal face takes heat slowly and softens late; a wooden one warms fast but burns its holder less. Shift on the shield's tooltip says where the line is.

## Fittings

Beyond the parts of the pattern, a finished tool takes optional **fittings**, one stack at a time at a bench (the way a tip or lining is fitted):

| Fitting | On | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| binding | sword, hand hammer, hand knife, bow, crossbow, and the tools that already had one | Lashing where head meets handle: takes some of the shock. |
| pommel | axe, maul, spear, pickaxe | A knob, ball or counterweight at the butt that moves the weight toward your hand. |
| tip | maul, hand hammer, and the tools that already had one | A hard piece set in the head. |
| spring | maul, spear, sword | Wire or rod of metal. Right click cocks it, the next blow lets it go for extra damage; a sword's release also lunges you forward; on a spear it lets the head be thrown from the spring. Stored energy is its volume times its stiffness; a spring worked past what its head can take takes a permanent set and finally snaps. |
| lanyard | sword, axe, maul, spear | A chain to the hand. Thrown, it reels what it hits and pulls the weapon weakly back at a cost in throw speed; held, a disarming shot cannot strip it. |
| edge guard | axe, spear, pickaxe, shovel | A plate strip down the haft that stiffens it, at a cost in mass. |
| magazine | crossbow | A box of plate or plank: its volume sets how many bolts a load takes, scattered, slower to load. |
| rest | bow, crossbow | A padded rest: steadier aim. |
| stabiliser | bow, crossbow | A counter-mass slug: steadier, slower to raise. |
| laminate | bow | Horn on the limbs: more energy for the length, weaker in rain or water. |
| boss | shield | A plate dome: a turned blow bashes and shoves the attacker, at a cost in mass and a weak spot. |
| strap | shield | String or wire carrying the weight: the face holds a little more. |
