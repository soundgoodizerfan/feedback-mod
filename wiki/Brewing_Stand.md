# Brewing Stand

## What vanilla did

The Brewing Stand takes a bottle of water in each of three slots, an ingredient above them and blaze powder as fuel, and after a fixed time turns the bottles into the potion that ingredient's recipe names. Every potion is a fixed recipe: nether wart for an awkward potion, then one more ingredient for the effect. Redstone lengthens it and glowstone strengthens it.

## What Feedback does

Feedback **replaces** the Brewing Stand. A vanilla one found in the world, in a village or placed by a command turns into this one, and the vanilla recipe is gone, as are the vanilla potion items and tipped arrows.

The stand is a pot of water over a heat source: a [Firebox](Firebox.md), a [Campfire](Campfire.md) or lava below it. The water pins at boiling, so the working range is room temperature to 100 Tu.

### Making a potion

Right-clicking the stand with anything, or nothing, opens its screen: a parchment map in the middle, a status panel on the left, and on the right a water slot, six ingredient slots, three bottle slots and the Cook button. Your inventory is below. Everything moves in and out by the screen's slots; clicking the block with an item does nothing else.

1. **Fill it.** A water bucket in the water slot fills the pot with three bottles' worth, 250 mB a bottle, and leaves the empty bucket. Nothing dilutes.
2. **Add ingredients.** Put up to six in the ingredient slots. Each one you add puts its line on the end of the path, in the order you added them, and you can add more at any time, even while stirring. An ingredient steers the brew by the compounds it carries, never by what it is: anything with the right compounds works, and a modded plant with the same compounds behaves the same. The cut an ingredient carries from the [Prep Table](Prep_Table.md) is its grind: a whole one gives up its compounds slowly, a sliced one faster, a minced one fastest. A rotten plant gives up less and pulls the brew toward a hazard. Hovering an ingredient draws a ghost line on the map from the end of the path to where adding it would take the flask, with the same cut and the pot's heat now. The line is dashed by grind: a whole piece is mostly gap, a sliced one half and half, a minced one solid.
3. **Stir.** Moving the mouse in circles over the map walks the marker along the line. The white flask starts at the centre. Each ingredient is a line from where the marker stands, as long as what comes out of it: the cut and the pot's heat at the moment it goes in set the length, and from then the line is fixed and never grows. Below about 30 Tu nothing comes out at all. The flask moves only when you stir, pour, hit the wall or ride a vortex, and when stirring it follows the drawn line exactly. The line the flask has walked stays behind it. You can stop short of the end; an ingredient you do not reach is spent and adds nothing. When the flask has walked a whole line, that ingredient is in the water and its slot empties, free for another. Only the ingredient being stirred in right now is dimmed and cannot be taken back; any other can be taken out, and its line leaves the path.
4. **Cook.** The Cook button ends the brew where the flask is. The pot must be at least 70 Tu. A flask inside an effect node makes that potion, and a flask anywhere else makes a **dud draught**. Either way the water and every ingredient are spent. Tip out empties the pot and spends what is in it.
5. **Take the bottles.** The finished bottles appear in the three bottle slots.

### The map

The map is a plane. Each compound pulls in its own direction, and compounds come in opposing pairs: sugar against bitter alkaloids, stimulants against sedatives, minerals against volatile oils, toxins against resins. Classes also differ in how far one dose carries the flask: vitamins and proteins pull a little and serve for fine correction, sugars and resins carry moderately, minerals, alkaloids, toxins and sedatives further, and stimulants and volatile oils furthest, so the near nodes take three slots and the far ones five or six. Conductive and luminous minerals (redstone and glowstone) tighten a near miss toward the nearest node along one axis each and never rescue a wild one.

Landing inside a node makes its effect: the flask's centre must be inside the node's ring. The further you are from the node's centre **across** the map, the weaker the potion; the further **up or down**, the shorter it lasts. A node's strongest potions sit past rarer or opposed compounds.

**Fog.** The map is covered in fog. The flask uncovers a small area as it passes, and that stays uncovered for you. Hazards, vortices and nodes you have not made show only where the fog is gone. A faint dashed line runs from the centre to every node, through the fog, so a hidden one can be found by following its line. Each line ends in the node's icon: a bottle silhouette with a question mark for a node you have not made (the same shape as the flask, so you can match them), the effect's own icon for one you have. A few fixed outcrop marks show through the fog to steer by. The map is yours: another player uncovers their own.

**The compass.** The frame round the map is a trim band with a dot for every class of compound, placed where a line from the middle of the map at that class's angle meets the map's edge, so you can see where a food will take you before it goes in. Each dot is the class's colour and has a short name beside it, cut to fit the band. Opposing classes sit opposite one another. The trim does not move when you zoom or pan, and nothing of it is drawn on the map. Hovering an ingredient enlarges the dots of the classes it carries, more for the stronger; hovering a dot or its name lists the class in full and the items in your inventory and the stand that carry it. The compass is never fogged.

**The flask has a size.** It touches a zone or a vortex when its edge does, not only its centre, and it is drawn that size on the map, growing and shrinking as you zoom. A node needs the flask's centre inside its ring.

**The wall.** The edge of the map pushes the flask back and costs nothing, so a flask driven into it slides along it.

**View.** The mouse wheel over the map zooms about the cursor, and dragging with the right or middle button pans it, never past the wall. R puts the view back on the whole map. Stirring is the same at any zoom, since it is read from how fast the cursor circles the middle of the view.

**Stir pace.** How fast you stir matters near a hazard. A slow stir moves the flask in fine steps and spends little path in a zone; a fast stir covers ground but loses more potency for each bit of path it crosses there.

**Pour.** The Pour button ladles water over the brew and sends the flask a fixed step straight toward the centre, at the price of a slice of the brew's potency. It is the only way to undo an overshoot.

### Hazards

- **Precipitate zones** (patches of fallen solids) pull the compounds out of the water. While the flask is inside, the brew loses potency; outside, it recovers. Drained to nothing, the brew fails.
- **Scorch zones** (charred patches) are harmless while the pot is cool and drain potency only above their temperature.
- **Vortices** (spirals) spin once the pot is hot. A spinning vortex turns a flask inside it, and a flask that reaches its centre jumps to a fixed place, which shows on your map from then on.
- **Heat only makes scorch zones bite.** An over-hot pot moves nothing; a hot pot also spins the vortices.

### A failed brew teaches

A dud leaves a **residue mark** on your map where the brew ended (or where a zone drained it away). Hovering the mark says what the nearest node you have not made *smells of*: the class of compound that node leans on, such as resin. It never names the effect. The dud draught itself tastes of that class too. The mark stays until you make that node's potion.

### Potions

A bottle of potion carries the effect, its level and length from where the brew landed, and a **potency** that fades. A bottle loses potency over a few days, faster in the warm and slower in the cold, and a faded potion is weaker, not shorter. A bottle that has faded away has no effect. Drinking applies the effect; every bottle can be drunk. The fire potion sets the drinker alight for the bottle's length times one plus its level. The explosion potion releases its heat at the drinker: two and a half times the thrown damage at the centre (10 at level I, 20 at II, 30 at III, against 20 health) and ten seconds alight. Do not drink it unprotected: fire resistance and armour answer the heat, and no blocks are blown out.  A dud draught gives a short nausea, and its colour leans toward the class of the nearest node you have not made (plain water-blue when there is none). Each class has a colour, shown on its compass dot.

### Throwing

Sneaking and using a bottle throws it. It breaks where it lands and its potion reaches every creature within about two and a half blocks (the spread of a 250 mB bottle), the nearer the longer: the level stays, the length falls off with distance, and the potency left in the bottle applies as when drunk. A dud draught cannot be thrown. See [Thrown and imbued potions](Thrown_and_Imbued_Potions.md).

### Lacing food

Any potion can be poured into food. Pour a bottle into a [stockpot](Stockpot.md) with something in it, or sneak and use a ladled serving or a dish with the bottle in your other hand. The bottle is spent and the food carries the potion's effect at its level, hidden. A pot shares the bottle over all its servings, so each carries a shorter dose and a quieter taste; a dud draught will not mix in, and food takes one potion.

Eating a laced food gives the effect through the same path as a meal's own buffs: fat and fibre delay it. Drinking a potion plainly ignores taste, but in food the taste can give it away. Every compound class has a taste, and the potion's taste is the mean of what went in. If the potion's taste is far from the food's own and loud enough, you notice something wrong with the food as you eat; a close or weak taste goes unnoticed. A fine palate lowers both thresholds. A nibble (sneak and tap the food) reports a faint aftertaste of the compound class the taste leans on when it stands out the same way.

### More nodes

- **Explosion** (up past the stimulants, at about the top right of the map; five slots reach it: two chile peppers, two cocoa cookies and a honey, minced, with the vortex passed below 80 Tu) is a fast release of heat. Thrown, creatures in reach take heat damage that falls off with distance and are set alight; drunk or eaten, the drinker takes it whole, and flammable blocks beside the burst catch. No blocks are blown out. Fire resistance answers the heat.
- **Fire** (far east, past sugar and mineral; five slots reach it: three honey, a kelp and a sugar, minced, though the run clips the east precipitate patch, and a magma cream added second plus another sugar make six that clear it) sets creatures alight when thrown, and the drinker when drunk.
- **Frost** (far west, past the alkaloids and toxins; five slots reach it: a nether wart and four poisonous potatoes, minced, crossing the scorch patch only if the pot is over 90 Tu) is slowness; it can be drunk or thrown.
- **Featherstep** (west, a little above the middle, past the volatile oils; five slots reach it: three ginger, a poisonous potato and a chile pepper, minced, crossing the scorch patch only if the pot is over 90 Tu) is slow falling that also holds you up on powder snow, as leather boots do; sneak to sink. It does not spare you from pressure plates.
- **Heat ward** (south, below the middle; three slots reach it: two kelp and a pufferfish, minced) is fire resistance with a price: while it lasts, cold bites harder. Frost builds half again as fast in powder snow and freezing damage is half again as large.
- **Smelter's blood** (south-east, below the resistance node; five slots reach it: a honey and four kelp, minced, the kelp first so the line stays clear of the east precipitate) thickens bare skin: a bare hand holds a piece 150 Tu hotter before it hurts. Hotter pieces still burn. The price is a dulled hand: swinging and digging run 15% slower while it lasts.
- **Quench** (far north-west, past featherstep and the volatile oils; ginger and garlic fall short, and four dragon breath, minced, reach it) is a cool damp breeze round the drinker. While it lasts, hot machines and cables within five blocks lose heat to the room four times as fast, and hot workpieces in your pack cool four times as fast; a fire under a vessel still heats it. The breeze draws warmth from you too: frost builds a third faster in powder snow. It can be drunk, not thrown. See [Heat](Heat.md).
- **Antivenom** (south-west, below the poison node; four slots reach it: four pufferfish, minced, the run crossing the south-west scorch zone, harmless under 90 Tu) is a ward: while it lasts only two fifths of poison damage gets through, whether the poison came from fumes, bad food or a sting. It is never immunity, and it has a price: the binder thickens the blood, so stamina refills at seven tenths of its usual rate. It can be drunk, not thrown.
- **Radiation ward** (far south-west, past antivenom and frost; six slots reach it: four poisonous potatoes and two spider eyes, minced, the run crossing the south-west scorch zone, harmless under 90 Tu) is a ward: while it lasts a little over half of the [radiation](Radiation.md) dose you take each interval gets through. A scavenger binds the radicals but does not stop the rays, so it is never more than a half, and it has a price: the scavenger spends the body's repair, so every heal, from regeneration to a meal, lands at three fifths. It can be drunk, not thrown.
- **Static ground** (right of the middle and a little below it, past the east precipitate; five slots: honey, magma cream, sugar, ghast tear, kelp, all minced, in that order) is a ward: while it lasts only two fifths of electric shock damage gets through, never none. It has a price: the drinker is a conductor, so metal held in either hand warms steadily, up to about 120 Tu, and cools again once put away. Held attuned metal can lose its attunement if it warms past its attuning point. It can be drunk, not thrown.
- **Second wind** (far north, past the stimulants, a little right of straight up) refills stamina faster and after a shorter pause while it lasts. When it ends the stimulant is paid back as **winded**: stamina does not refill at all for a few seconds, longer for a higher level. It can be drunk, not thrown. Four slots reach it: two blaze powder and two chile peppers, minced and added into a hot pot, run north. The run passes the vortex, which spins only above 80 Tu, so a pot held between 70 and 80 Tu carries the flask through.
- **Fine palate** (far west, above frost; six slots reach it: three ginger, a nether wart and two poisonous potatoes, minced) sharpens the tongue. While it lasts, a laced food is noticed at a lower taste threshold, 40% lower for both how loud and how foreign the taste must be, so a faint or food-like taste still slips past. The price is bitterness: a meal gives a quarter less saturation. It can be drunk, not thrown.
- **Adrenaline** (north, a little left of straight up, between the stimulants and the volatile oils) halves the stamina every cast technique costs while it lasts, and the body does not heal: regeneration, a full belly and every other heal are refused. It can be drunk, not thrown. Four slots reach it, minced into a hot pot: a blaze powder and a chile pepper run north, and two ginger bend the line to the left. The run passes the vortex, which spins only above 80 Tu.
- **Steady hand** (far south, a little left of straight down, past the sedatives) halves a gun's spread and camera kick while it lasts; it never steadies them to nothing. It can be drunk, not thrown. Fermented spider eyes carry sedative and toxin, which together head south-west; a few kelp, whose sedative runs straight south and whose mineral drifts east, cancel the sideways drift. Five slots reach it, minced into a hot pot: kelp, fermented spider eye, kelp, then two pufferfish, whose toxin pulls the line back to the right. The run south skirts the scorch patch to the south-west.
- **Quickdraw** (east, between sugar and the stimulants, a little above speed) shortens every gun reload to four fifths at level I and three fifths at level II. When it ends the stimulant is paid back as mining fatigue for a few seconds, longer for a higher level, which slows every gun timer. It can be drunk, not thrown. Five slots reach it, minced into a hot pot: a chile pepper, then three honey and a sugar. The chile pepper runs north-east and the sweet ingredients carry the marker east to it; the run passes the vortex, which spins only above 80 Tu. Sugar added first runs along the precipitate patch to the south-east and loses potency there.
- **Assay** (west, a little above the alkaloid axis, between the alkaloids and the featherstep node) makes every readout resolve at the exact figure for about a minute: Jade tooltips, helmet and probe readouts and food figures show what the world holds, signed *Assay*. It is a look, not a tool: it does not replace carrying an instrument, which still gives everything else it does, and the world never reads your readings. The fine focus costs breadth: stamina refills a fifth slower while it lasts. It can be drunk, not thrown. Five slots reach it, minced into a pot: three ginger, a poisonous potato and a sliced poisonous potato. The nether wart and poisonous potato alkaloids pull west and the ginger lifts the run to it.
- **Speed** (east, a little above the middle, past sugar; three slots reach it: two honey and a sugar, minced, though the shortest order clips the east precipitate patch) grants Speed up to level III for about three minutes.
- **Strength** (north, a little left of straight up, past the volatile oils and stimulants; six slots reach it: a chile pepper, a cocoa cookie and four ginger, minced, the run passing the north-east vortex, harmless with the pot between 70 and 80 Tu) grants Strength up to level II for about ninety seconds.
- **Regeneration** (north-east, between the stimulants and sugar; four slots reach it: two blaze powder, a honey and a sugar, minced, the run passing the north-east vortex, harmless with the pot between 70 and 80 Tu) grants Regeneration up to level II for about forty-five seconds.
- **Resistance** (south-east, past sugar and minerals; four slots reach it: two kelp, a ghast tear and a honey, minced, crossing no hazard) grants Resistance up to level II for about three minutes.
- **Night vision** (north, just right of straight up, past the stimulants; three slots reach it: a chile pepper and two cocoa cookies, minced, the run passing the north-east vortex, harmless with the pot between 70 and 80 Tu) grants Night Vision for about five minutes.
- **Poison** (south-west, past the toxins; three slots reach it: a poisonous potato and two pufferfish, minced, the run crossing the south-west scorch zone, harmless under 90 Tu) poisons the drinker, or creatures it is thrown at, up to level II for about forty-five seconds. A tool soaked in it carries it into its blows.
