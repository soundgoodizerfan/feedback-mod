# Soldering Iron

The hand route to a joint: patience instead of a [Reflow Oven](Reflow_Oven.md).

## Crafting

```
C
I
S
```

C is a Copper Rod, I a Wrought Iron Rod and S a Stick.

## Using it

1. **Right-click a fire** (a [Firebox](Firebox.md), say) with the iron to heat it. It keeps its heat and cools slowly, so a [Thermometer](Thermometer.md) will tell you where it is.
2. Hold a pasted [Trace Board](Trace_Board.md) in the other hand and **right-click**. A soldering screen opens with a row of pads, one pair per mounted part (two on a plain board).
3. **Hold the mouse button and drag the tip along the row**, first pad to last, then let go. Each pad is graded on how hot it got (see [Solder](Solder.md)).

Pace is the skill. Go too fast and a pad stays **dull**: a cold joint. Linger and it goes **dark**: the flux burns, and if it keeps cooking the trace lifts. A **shiny** pad is a good joint. The iron cools as it works, so later pads want a slower pace; leave the screen (button up) to reheat at a fire and start again. Staying off the trace bridges solder to the next pad: it adds noise to a circuit, nothing worse. Close the screen with the button held and the tip counts as resting on the last pad for a while. A [Thermometer](Thermometer.md) on the iron gives the numbers. The iron never wears. Leaded solder poisons you while you work it.
