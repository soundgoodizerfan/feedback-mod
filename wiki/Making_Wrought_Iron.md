# Making Wrought Iron

A path from iron ore to wrought iron, the soft tough iron that plates, rods and most machine parts are made of. Iron ore, raw iron and vanilla's [iron ore](Iron_Ore.md) do not smelt to an ingot in any furnace or crucible: the ore first becomes a spongy **Iron Bloom** full of slag, and the slag is hammered out.

## What you need

- A hot, blown fire in a [Crucible](Crucible.md) or a [furnace](Furnaces.md) that reaches **1350 Tu**; see [Heat](Heat.md). A [Thermometer](Instruments.md#thermometer) helps.
- Iron ore, raw iron, or an iron mineral (hematite, magnetite, limonite) in any form: lump, crushed, dust or concentrate. See [Ore Processing](Ore_Processing.md) to prepare the ore.
- **Charcoal**.
- A [Hammer](Hammer.md) and an [Anvil](Anvil.md), with a [Hearth](Hearth.md) beside it, or a [Mechanical Hammer](Mechanical_Hammer.md).

## The route

1. **Make a bloom.** Put the iron ore and a piece of charcoal in the vessel and hold it between **1000 and 1350 Tu**, fastest near 1200. The batch takes a while, and a temperature that climbs too fast stalls it. The result is an **Iron Bloom**. Its tooltip says how much slag is left in it: *Clean*, *a little*, *plenty* or *packed*; a bloom from ore is packed.
2. **Do not overheat it.** Above **1400 Tu** the iron takes up carbon instead and becomes **cast iron**. Melting bare ore without charcoal gives **molten cast iron** the same way.
3. **Hammer the slag out**, hot. Either [shingle it by hand](#shingling-at-the-anvil) or put it under the Mechanical Hammer ([below](#under-the-mechanical-hammer)). A good job gives a **Wrought Iron Ingot** and a lump of **Bloomery Slag**.
4. **Reuse the slag.** Bloomery Slag still holds iron. Crush it and melt the grains for pig iron ([Ore Processing](Ore_Processing.md)).

## Shingling at the anvil

Put the hot bloom in the **center** of the [Anvil](Anvil.md), a hammer in the hammer slot, and press **Shingle**. The bloom is drawn as a billet with dark **slag pockets** in it.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Left click | A light blow. |
| Right click | A heavy blow. |
| `R` | Turn the piece a quarter turn. |
| Space | Put the hammer down and finish. |

Each blow pushes pockets out toward the nearest edge, wider than it is tall, so turn the piece to herd the rest. A pocket that passes the edge is gone. Blows only work while the piece is hot, and the slag freezes in place when the piece cools: keep it warm with a Hearth.

- **Done.** Finish with only a fifth of the pockets left or fewer and you get the ingot and the slag.
- **Not done.** Finish with more left and you get the bloom back with the slag it still holds. Heat it and work it again.
- **Too heavy.** A heavy blow harder than the iron can take splits the piece, and each split costs part of the iron.
- **Burnt.** Holding the piece over about **1400 Tu** for too long burns it to **Burnt Iron**.
- **Scale.** Each time you reheat a bloom you did not finish, a nugget's worth of iron is lost as scale. Nine such reheats burn the bloom.

The hammer wears as you work.

## Under the Mechanical Hammer

Put a hot bloom on the anvil of a [Mechanical Hammer](Mechanical_Hammer.md) and it drives the slag out unattended, giving a Wrought Iron Ingot and a Bloomery Slag. The hammer only works the bloom while it is inside its hot window (**900 to 1500 Tu**); a cooled bloom is left alone.

## Puddling

A cast iron batch becomes a bloom with little slag in the [Puddling Furnace](Puddling_Furnace.md), which a shaft stirs. That bloom needs far less shingling.

## Related ore steps

- **Hematite** roasts to **magnetite** of the same form (lump, crushed, concentrate or dust) with charcoal between **600 and 800 Tu**; above 1000 it burns to Burnt Iron. Magnetite answers a [magnet](Electromagnet.md#separating-ore).
- **Limonite** dehydrates to **hematite** of the same form between **300 and 600 Tu**.
- **Iron loose rock** on the surface drops cast iron nuggets.
