# Mechanical Press

A stamping press: a few very heavy strokes instead of many light hammer blows. It makes the same things a [hammer](Mechanical_Hammer.md) does (plate, foil, scrap, bullets), but it runs straight off a shaft without a linkage.

## Crafting

```
I I I
I B I
  S
```

I is a Wrought Iron Ingot, B is a [Bearing](Bearing.md) and S is a [Shaft](Shaft.md).

## Using it

It sits **directly on a shaft line**; connect it from any side. It presses once per turn's worth of rotation, so faster shafts press more often.

- **Right-click with an item** to put a workpiece in (or swap it).
- **Right-click with an empty hand** to take it out.
- **Right-click with a [Machine Head](Machine_Head.md)** to seat it (the old one comes back); **sneak and right-click with an empty hand** to pull it. With no head the press does nothing and says so.

## Things to know

- Every stroke is the seated head's **full ceiling** (24 St for an iron head of four ingots), enough for steel (2 Fu a stroke) without any gearing. That also means it **overshoots soft metals easily**: 24 Fu a stroke on copper goes from ingot to foil in one.
- It doesn't stop when the plate is done. Plates become foil, foil becomes scrap. See [Metalworking](Metalworking.md).
- Steel still has to be hot.
- A stroke that achieves nothing (metal too cold, scrap already) wears the **head** (not the press); replace a spent head to reset it.
- It's a **90 Su** load, one of the heaviest you can put on a shaft network.

## Press or hammer?

The hammer is cheap, gentle and adjustable, but needs a linkage and attention. The press is expensive and blunt, but simple to drive. Pick the press when you want force without building a gear train; pick the hammer when you need a light touch.
