# Blueprint Table

A table for drawing a tool, weapon, shield, bow or piece of armour on paper and **reading its numbers before you make it**. Guns are drawn at the [Gun Stand](Gun_Stand.md) instead.

## Crafting

```
P P
T T
```

P is Paper and T is a Crafting Table.

## Using it

Right-click it to open the drawing. Pick:

- a **type** (pickaxe, chestplate, bow...),
- one of its **patterns**: which optional parts it has (binding, guard, tip, studs) and how long its handle is,
- a **material for each part** (each offers every material that fits that part),
- the **edge**, on a tool that has one.

The screen shows the design's figures, **exactly**. Requirements and plans are free; what a finished piece *has* still costs an instrument ([Balance Scale](Balance_Scale.md), [Scratch Files](Scratch_Files.md)).

**Print** turns a sheet of paper into a **Blueprint** carrying the design and its figures. Put a Blueprint back on the table to open it for drawing again. The table keeps nothing; the paper comes back to you when you close it.

## What a blueprint is for

Mostly it's a plan to look at. The one thing that **needs** one is the [crossbow](Tools_and_Weapons.md#crossbow-blueprint), the only tool with a mechanism whose parts must fit each other: put any Crossbow Blueprint in the grid with the parts. The craft keeps it. The parts don't have to match the drawing. Repairs and re-forges never need one.
