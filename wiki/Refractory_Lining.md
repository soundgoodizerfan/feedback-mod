# Refractory Lining

A thicker brick wall that you fit inside a [Crucible](Crucible.md). It makes the crucible **heat up and cool down more slowly**. It does **not** make it any hotter.

## Crafting

Four Bricks in a square.

```
B B
B B
```

## Fitting it

Right-click a Small or Large Crucible with the lining. It goes inside, so it doesn't matter which face you click. A crucible takes **one** lining. Breaking the crucible gives the lining back.

## What it actually does

A lined crucible behaves like a heavier crucible:

- **Slower to heat up.** It takes longer (and more fuel) to reach temperature.
- **Slower to cool down.** Pull the fire away and it holds its heat much longer.
- **Steadier.** Short flickers in the fire barely show up in the crucible's temperature. That helps a lot with processes that go wrong if the temperature swings or climbs too fast.
- **Slower to respond to a thermostat.** A [Bimetallic Strip](Bimetallic_Strip.md) or [Controller](Controller.md) driving it will overshoot a bit more, because the crucible keeps rising after the heat is cut.

What it **won't** do is raise the crucible's final temperature. A crucible still settles just under the temperature of the fire beneath it, lined or not. To get hotter you need a hotter fire. See [Powered Bellows](Powered_Bellows.md).

## Small vs. large

On a **Small Crucible** the difference is dramatic: it roughly doubles how sluggish it is, which puts it partway to a Large Crucible's steadiness without the size. On a **Large Crucible**, which is already very heavy, you'll barely notice it.

## When to use it

- **Yes:** long holds at a steady temperature, or jobs that are ruined by heating too fast.
- **No:** jobs where you need the crucible to get hot *quickly*, or to cool down fast between batches. Leave the small crucible bare for those.
