# Designations

A **designation** is the name a finished thing earns from what it is made of and how it is built. A gun is a revolver, an axe head and a spear head on one haft make a halberd, a bowl of broth and noodles is a ramen. Nothing is chosen from a menu: the name is read off the finished item by rule, the moment it exists, so a build you improvised can still turn out to be a known thing.

A designation never changes what an item does. The perks come only from the parts, the bands and the shapes; a name is how you and the game call it. You can write your own name on a blueprint at the [Blueprint Table](Blueprint_Table.md).

Every list below is read top to bottom, and **the first row that fits is the name**. A row lists every condition that must hold; a condition that gives several words ("normal or long") accepts any one of them. A build that fits no row is named by its size, shape and material: size words for the bands that are not normal, then the shape and material, then the type, as in "Wide Serrated Iron Sword" or "Long Scale Iron Chestplate". A name never repeats a word its rule already needs: a Dagger is not a "Short Dagger". A hard tip or stud set into the part goes in front ("Iron-tipped Steel Sword").

A **band** is a range an axis sits in. Armor and weapon parts have axes you aim at the bench; the band the finished part lands in is what the rows below test. ## Food

A single food is named from its material, its cut, whether it is raw, cooked or dried, and how salted or smoked it is. A dish is named from its parts: its container, and what sits in each of its main, base and side parts. A dish's parts vote for a cuisine through their materials (tomato leans Italian and Mexican, soy and ginger East Asian, potato and cabbage Eastern European, noodles Italian and East Asian, rice East Asian and Mexican); a tie lets every leading cuisine try its names in order. A name in brackets is filled in with the name of that part ("Beef Kebab"). Bread is named under [Bread](Food.md#bread). Stale, spoiled, burnt and chunky foods get a word in front; see [Food](Food.md#naming).
