# Hand Tools

The hand tools are the ones you carry and use yourself: a hammer, a file and a drill for shaping metal in your hands, a screwdriver for the work at the [Engineering Table](Engineering_Table.md), a wrench for turning blocks, a pry bar for prying, the knife, spatula and spoon for food, and the saw for wood and stone. Most have a head or blade shaped at a bench and assembled with a handle; a few are hot-hammered from heated metal in a crafting grid. Each has its own page.

- [Hammer](Hammer.md): shapes plates and stock held in your other hand, and is the tool the others are hammered with. Made in a crafting grid from cobblestone and a stick, or a better head shaped at a bench and put on a haft. The hammer, the knife, the file, the drill and the screwdriver are all [hafted tools](Hafted_Tool.md): a haft with a face of that kind.
- [File](#file) and [Screwdriver](#screwdriver): the tools of the Engineering Table's file and driver work. Each is a head on a handle. The file also cuts a rod in your hands.
- [Hand Drill](#hand-drill): drills a plate held in your other hand.
- [Hand Knife](Prep_Table.md): cuts food and dough on the Prep Table. Its blade is one piece with a length and a width; a harder edge can be set in its edge slot. The blade can be forged at the Anvil as a skinning, utility or tanto blade ([what each does](Tools_and_Weapons.md#how-each-one-fights)). Made by shaping its blade hot at the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) or [Anvil](Anvil.md), then putting it together with a handle in a crafting grid.
- [Mortar](Brewing_Stand.md#mortar): an ingredient of the [Brewing Stand](Brewing_Stand.md), whose bowl crushes plants. Made from a stick and stone in a crafting grid.
- [Sawblade](Sawblade.md): saws a log or plank block you look at while you hold right-click, and goes in the Stonecutter's saw slot to cut stone and wood. Made by casting a saw blade in a [Saw Blade Mold](Casting.md#tool-part-molds), then putting it together in a crafting grid.
- [Spatula](#spatula): flips food in a [Skillet](Skillet.md). Made from a spatula blade shaped from plate, planks or bone (metal is shaped hot), put with a handle.
- [Wrench](Wrench.md): turns a placed block a quarter turn. Made by hot-hammering four ingots of one hard metal in a crafting grid with a hammer.
- [Pry Bar](Pry_Bar.md): pries fittings off a machine and lifts nailed or pinned blocks off whole. A pry head on a haft.
- [Spoon](#spoon): stirs and skims a [Stockpot](Stockpot.md), and tastes a dish. Made from a spoon bowl shaped from plate, planks or bone (metal is shaped hot), put with a handle.
- [Tongs](Hazards.md#tongs): hold hot work so it doesn't burn your hand. Made from two rods of one metal at working temperature, a grip and a hammer, in a crafting grid. They clamp one piece, which the hammer, file and drill can work in the jaws ([how](Hazards.md#clamping-a-piece)).

## Working in your hands

The hammer, file and drill all work a piece the same way: the tool in one hand, the piece in the other (or clamped in [Tongs](Hazards.md#clamping-a-piece)), **hold right-click**. In first person the piece is shown in front of your view while you work, since your other hand is empty. It dips and tilts on every stroke, and glows by its temperature when it is hot. With reduce motion on, it does not dip. The piece is not shown when you work at a vice (the vice shows its own piece) or in third person.

## File

A **File** is a toothed bar on a handle. Shape a **File Head** from a plate or an ingot at a bench (the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) for metal), then put it with a haft (a stick, a rod or bone) in a crafting grid; it is a [hafted tool](Hafted_Tool.md) whose face is a file head. The head alone is a file too. The head's material sets how long the file lasts.

The head comes in three **cuts**. A **bastard cut** takes two grains a stroke, a **second cut** one, a **smooth cut** half a grain. Coarse strokes leave tool marks on the part; a part left with enough marks reads one flaw worse. Each stroke of a smooth file burnishes the marks of a coarser one away. A longer head takes more per stroke and wanders more (its **length** axis).

A file also works parts at a bench: carried in your inventory, it takes a grain off an axis of a made part at the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md#filing) and every other part bench. Put a file in a tool slot of the [Engineering Table](Engineering_Table.md). Recipes that ask for a file (the [Calipers](Instruments.md#calipers), the [Scratch File Set](Instruments.md#scratch-file-set), a [Saw Head or Plasma Head](Power_Cutter.md)) wear it a point.

### Filing a rod

Hold a hafted file in one hand and **one rod** in the other, then **hold right-click**. The file takes the rod and lands a stroke about every third of a second, wearing the file a point and making you a little hungrier each time. The file goes on until you let go, swap to another item, or no stroke can cut; letting go hands the rod back as it is. Stroke by stroke the rod is cut into a **Screw** or **Machine Bolt** ([Fasteners](Fasteners.md)) or a gun **Bolt** ([Gun Parts](Gun_Parts.md)), the same pieces the [Lathe](Lathe.md) and [Grinding Wheel](Grinding_Wheel.md) make, at about twice their work. A coarser cut takes more per stroke. Only metal softer than the file's edge can be filed.

A piece clamped in a [Vice](Vice.md) is worked the same way with the tool in one hand and the off hand free.

## Hand Drill

A **Hand Drill** is a bit on a long haft. Shape a **Drill Tip** from a rod or an ingot at a bench (the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) for metal), then put it with a long haft and a tool binding in a crafting grid; it is a [hafted tool](Hafted_Tool.md) whose face is a drill tip. A harder piece can be set in the tip's edge slot. The bit's material sets how long the drill lasts.

Hold the drill in one hand and **one plate** in the other, then **hold right-click**. Each stroke turns the bit into the plate, about every third of a second, until it is a **Drilled Plate**; a harder plate takes more strokes. Letting go hands the plate back as it is. The bit wears one point per stroke, and each stroke makes you a little hungrier.

**The bit must be harder than the plate.** A softer bit cuts nothing. Drilled plates are the stock of the bullet, ball, atomizing and tube molds and of gun parts, so a hand drill opens them before the [Machine Age](Ages.md); the [Drill Press](Drill_Press.md) does the same work on a shaft.

The bit comes in two shapes:

- **Twist flute**: clears its chips and bites on with every turn.
- **Spade flute**: takes less from each turn.

## Screwdriver

A **Screwdriver** is a hardened bit on a handle. Shape a **Driver Bit** from a rod or an ingot at a bench, then put it with a handle (a stick, a rod or bone) in a crafting grid. The bit alone is a screwdriver too. The bit's material sets how long the screwdriver lasts.

The tip comes in three shapes, each better or worse on each form of fastener ([Fasteners](Fasteners.md)). A **cross tip** suits screws and rounds a machine bolt's head. A **hex tip** grips a machine bolt and slips in a screw. A **flat tip** is middling on screws and slips a little on bolts. Rivets are the same for all three. A good fit starts the fastener at a better quality; a poor one starts it worse. A longer **shank** reaches the seat square and starts the fastener better, but twists and wears the tool faster; a short one is stiff and lasts.

Put a screwdriver in a tool slot of the Engineering Table. Most machine recipes ask for one and wear it a point. The tip's fit moves the fasteners' quality, which sets the width of the green band in the [torque game](Engineering_Table.md#hand-work).

A **ratchet** is a turned rod fitted as a pawl on the handle. With it the torque game needs no regrip: each pull starts partly wound, still short of the band, and the band drifts less between pulls. A pawl of soft or weak metal slips over its teeth under a hard pull: the gauge stalls and clicks short of the band, so the pull cannot reach it. A stronger metal holds higher.

## Spatula

A modular hand tool for flipping the food in a [Skillet](Skillet.md).

Shape a **Spatula Blade** (from a plate, planks or bone; metal is shaped hot at the Smithing Table or [Anvil](Anvil.md)) and put it with a handle (stick or bone) in a crafting grid, as for any [modular tool](Tools_and_Weapons.md). The blade's shape is the choice:

| Blade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Turner** | Turns one piece. |
| **Slotted Turner** | Turns one piece and lets about a tenth of its moisture run off through the slots. |

The handle's strength changes the tool's durability a little: a weaker handle than wood's shortens it, a stronger one lengthens it, and wood is normal.

Right-click a piece in a skillet to flip it. The quarter under your crosshair is the one that turns. Only the face down chars, and a flip swaps the faces. Each flip wears the spatula one point. To lift the pan off the fire, use [Tongs](Hazards.md#lifting-a-skillet).

## Spoon

A modular hand tool for working a [Stockpot](Stockpot.md) and tasting a [dish](Prep_Table.md): hold it to stir, tap it to skim, use it against a dish in your other hand to taste.

Shape a **Spoon Bowl** (from a plate, planks or bone; metal is shaped hot at the Smithing Table or Anvil) and put it with a handle (stick or bone) at the [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md), as for any modular tool. The handle's strength changes the tool's durability a little: a weaker handle than wood's shortens it, a stronger one lengthens it, and wood is normal.

Aim at a stockpot with the spoon in hand.

- **Hold use to stir.** The stir runs at full pace for as long as you hold. It keeps the bottom from scorching and mixes the pot.
- **Tap use to skim.** A press shorter than about a third of a second lifts scum off the top. A skim straight after another does nothing.

Skim before you stir: stirring while scum is on top folds it into the pot for good. [Stockpot](Stockpot.md#stirring-and-skimming) has what stirring and scum do to the food.

A bigger bowl stirs harder and skims more per stroke. The bowl comes in two shapes:

- **Short spoon**: an ordinary bowl that stirs and skims as well as each other.
- **Ladle**: stirs less and scoops more, and its long reach keeps your hand off a hotter pot, so it stands a hotter pot than a short spoon before the handle burns you.

The **depth** axis trades the two: a deep bowl skims more and a shallow one stirs better. A deeper bowl takes more stock.

Stirring wears the spoon one point every 5 seconds; each skim that lifts scum wears it one point. A broken spoon does nothing. A pot hotter than the handle's material stands burns your hand once a second while you stir. See [Hazards](Hazards.md#hot-things).

### Tasting

Hold a dish in one hand and use the spoon with the other. Chat prints the dish's name, then one line per quality, the same qualities a villager judges in a [dish order](Villagers.md#dish-orders):

- heartiness (hearty or light), richness (lean or rich), chew (tender or chewy), moisture (dry or moist)
- doneness (raw or cooked), char (unburnt or burnt), saltiness, smokiness
- freshness (stale or fresh), warmth (cold or hot)
- the share of the dish that is meat, fish, greens and grain

Each reading is a word on a four-step scale. With an [instrument](Instruments.md) at its finest reading, each line also shows the exact figure. Rot, cooling and drying show in the reading as they are at that moment.

A bowl smaller than the standard size takes a small mouthful and tells only the two qualities furthest from the middle of their range; a bigger bowl tells all of them. A dish hotter than the handle's material stands, with the bowl's reach, burns your hand. Each tasting wears the spoon one point.
