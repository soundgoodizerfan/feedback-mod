# Spring Gun

The spring gun is a spring-piston air gun. It does not ignite anything, so it is not a [gun](Guns.md): a spring drives a piston that pushes air behind a pellet, and it throws the pellet nearly silently.

## Parts

Made at the [Tool Table](Tool_Table.md) from a **Blueprint** (draw one at the [Blueprint Table](Blueprint_Table.md), under Weapons):

| Part | Takes | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Receiver** | any material | The stock and frame. It rates how much the spring may push. |
| **Barrel** | a metal | Its bore and its length axis set how well the push turns into speed: a longer barrel keeps the push going; a wide bore wastes it. |
| **Piston** | a metal | Moves with the push; a heavy one steals some. |
| **Spring** | wire or rod of a metal (one to six) | Stores energy: its volume times its stiffness. |

## Using it

Hold use to **cock** it; it takes longer with a stiffer spring and a weaker wielder. A cocked gun stays cocked, and the tooltip says so. Use again to fire. The ammunition is **pellets**: any **metal nugget** in your inventory. A pellet is a plug of the bore, so its mass follows the metal's density: lead hits harder than iron. One nugget makes several pellets. With no nuggets a cocked gun clicks dry and stays cocked. The pellet flies like an arrow and cannot be picked up.

## Misuse

A spring that stores more than its receiver can take is **overloaded**: each cocking gives it a permanent set (it stores less afterwards), and far past the rating it snaps, wrecking part of the gun and hurting your hand.
