# Electric Gun

The electric gun throws a projectile with a battery's charge instead of burning powder, so it is not a [gun](Guns.md). It is made at the bench that suits the stock ([Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) for metal, [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md) for wood) from a **Blueprint** (draw one at the [Blueprint Table](Blueprint_Table.md), under Weapons), and needs the weapons treatise like the other weapons.

## Parts

| Part | Takes | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Barrel** | a conductor (a metal that carries current) | One of three kinds. Its conductor sets the current it carries before it wears. Axes: barrel length, bore, and for a coil, stages. |
| **Receiver** | any material | Holds the battery. Its insulation sets the voltage band it holds off. |
| **Grip** | stick, rod, bone or leather | The hand. |

### Barrels

- **Coil barrel:** windings round the bore. Each **stage** adds energy, but only as many as the barrel is long enough to fit work well; the rest are mostly wasted as switching losses. The slug must be a **magnetic** metal (iron, for one).
- **Rail barrel:** two rails the slug bridges. It takes what its conductor is rated for, and any conductor slug will do. A longer pair keeps the push.
- **Plasma barrel:** an arc channel. A shot vaporises a conductor nugget into a short bolt that burns what it hits and falls off with distance.

A built gun is named for what it is: a **coilgun**, a **gauss rifle** (a coil with many stages), a **railgun**, a **plasma gun**. The name gives no bonus.

## Using it

**Load a battery** by clicking the gun in your inventory with a [battery](Battery.md) on your cursor; click the gun with an empty cursor to take it out. The battery keeps its charge, so it goes back to a [Charger](Charger.md) as it is. Use fires. Each shot takes one metal nugget from your inventory and the shot's energy from the battery: a weak battery gives a weaker shot, never a refusal. Coil and rail slugs fly like arrows; a plasma bolt is instant and short-ranged.

Each shot warms the gun, and a warm gun recovers more slowly. A rail gives a crack, a coil a thump, and an arc of sparks leaves the muzzle.

## Misuse

Nothing wears from correct use. A battery whose voltage is over the receiver's band shocks you and wears the receiver. Firing in water or rain arcs to you. A current over what the barrel's conductor carries wears the barrel, and far over it the conductor fuses and wrecks the gun.
