# Foods

Foods and dishes are named from what they are and what was done to them, not from a list of recipes. Nothing is gated by a name: hunger, saturation, hazards and buffs come from the food's state exactly as before. The name is how you and the wiki call the thing.

## In vanilla

A food has one fixed name. Slicing, drying or mashing it does not exist.

## In Feedback

### One food, by what was done to it

- **Fries:** a root vegetable (potato) cut at the [Prep Table](Prep_Table.md) and cooked through.
- **Potato Chips:** the same, cut and then dried out.
- **Croutons:** a starchy food that is not a tuber (bread), cut and then dried or charred.

Stale, spoiled, burnt and chunky (a ragged cut) put a word in front of any name: "Stale Fries", "Burnt Croutons". Spoiled wins over stale, then burnt, then chunky.

### A dish, by its parts

A dish's parts vote for a cuisine through their materials (tomato leans Italian and Mexican, soy and ginger East Asian, potato and cabbage Eastern European, noodles Italian and East Asian, rice East Asian and Mexican). A tie lets every leading cuisine try its names in order. A name then fills in the part names:

- **Italian:** Pasta e (main), Pasta al (side, a sharp vegetable such as tomato).
- **East Asian:** Stir-fried (main) Noodles, (main) Rice Bowl.
- **Mexican:** Arroz con (main), (main) Burrito in a flatbread wrap, (main) Taco in a wrap with a sharp side.
- **Eastern European:** (potato) Pierogi in a wrap, Hunter's (main) with a mushroom, (root) Stew with a protein main and a beet or turnip.
- **No cuisine:** Noodles with (main); Mashed Potatoes (a minced tuber in a bowl); Salsa (a minced sharp vegetable in a bowl).

A soup carries no list of what went into the pot, so it is not named from its ingredients.

## Changed vanilla names

Potato and baked potato (Fries, Potato Chips, Mashed Potatoes); Bread (Croutons); the vanilla bread recipe is removed (see Bread).

## Bread

Vanilla's three-wheat bread recipe does not exist. Bread is a dish: formed raw at the [Prep Table](Prep_Table.md) from a dough, optional enrichments and a shape, then baked whole in a heated vessel like a pie. There is one Bread item; what it is called comes from what went in.

- **Dough** (the container): [kneaded dough](Dough.md) is best, but any dough forms. How it was kneaded scales the baked bread's hunger and saturation, and a glossy, crumbly or tough dough names the bread so.
- **Enrichment** (up to two, optional): an egg, sugar or honey worked in. A rich dough feeds more than a lean one.
- **Shape**: lay one dough in the Prep Table's Parts tab as a **Loaf**, **Flat** or **Roll**. Unshaped dough forms a loaf.

| Name | What makes it |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lean Bread** | a loaf of plain dough |
| **Enriched Bread** | a loaf with an egg, sugar or honey in it |
| **Flatbread**, **Enriched Flatbread** | a flat shape, plain or enriched |
| **Bread Roll**, **Enriched Roll** | a roll shape, plain or enriched |
| **Laminated Bread** | a dough layered with a fat; no fat food exists yet, so none can be made |

The raw bread feeds little and is called *Unbaked* until the heat has taken it; left on, it goes dry and then burnt like any food, and a loaf that sits goes stale and then spoils. A baked Flatbread food (dough laid flat and baked alone) is also a wrap's container.

Dry grain (wheat, rice) keeps far longer than cooked food, but it does spoil. A loaf goes stale and then spoils like any baked food.
