# Stockpot

A pot that cooks a batch. It is made of a material, and the material sets how fast the pot follows its fire.

## Crafting

Five plates of one material in a U, three along the bottom and one up each side:

```
 P   .   P
 P   P   P
```

P is a plate of one heat-conducting material. Each plate material makes its own pot. A heavy material follows its fire slowly; a light, well-conducting one follows it quickly. Blocks with no material behave as wrought iron. On an [Induction Stove](Induction.md#induction-stove) only a magnetic material heats.

## Using it

Set it on a lit [Firebox](Firebox.md) or an [Induction Stove](Induction.md#induction-stove), then:

- **Water bucket:** pour a bucket in (up to two). You get the empty bucket back.
- **A food:** add one (up to six). Raw or cooked, any food a [Skillet](Skillet.md) or [Furnace](Furnaces.md) would cook.
- **Salt:** shake a pinch into a pot that has something in it. It adds no volume and no food, only salt (as much as one salted food) and salt's own *Brine Tolerance* perk, so the servings tolerate more of what is live in them. A pot can only be so salty: past a saturated brine more salt buys nothing.
- **A bowl:** ladle one serving.
- **A [Spoon](Spoon.md):** hold to stir, tap to skim (below).
- **Empty hand:** read the pot (how many servings, how far along). Sneak with an empty hand to tip it out; a hot pot burns the hand.

The pot shows its contents. The water surface rises and falls with the water and shifts in color from broth to burnt. Pale blobs of scum float on top, swirl marks show while it is stirred, and food lies on the floor of the pot tinted raw, cooked or burnt. Stirring, skimming and simmering each make a sound. Jade shows the pot's temperature.

## What happens

The pot is one mixture, not a recipe. Whatever you put in is blended: it cooks, the live organisms in raw food die as it cooks through, and toxins stay. A batch of dense food cooks through more slowly than a batch of thin food.

- **Boiling holds the pot cool.** While there is water the pot stays at boiling, however hot the fire. A hard fire only boils the water away faster. Low and slow is safe. A pot boiled dry climbs to the fire's heat and scorches.
- **Toxin is a concentration.** Water and clean food dilute it; boiling the pot down concentrates it. Heat does not remove it. A poisonous potato in a big pot of water can be safe to eat; boil the water off and it isn't.
- **Servings follow volume.** Water has no food in it, so a thin pot makes many weak servings. A stew gives a little more than its ingredients eaten one by one, since nothing is wasted.
- **Ladle early.** Scorching takes time. Servings taken before it starts are fine.

## Stirring and skimming

The pot goes on cooking while you work it.

- **Stir** by holding use with a spoon aimed at the pot. Over a hard fire the bottom scorches even with water in the pot; steady stirring stops it. Over a gentle fire (a wood campfire is one) you need not stir at all; a soul campfire or a hot firebox is a hard fire. A stirred pot also cooks through faster, and its servings come out even; a pot left to stand lets its solids settle, so the first ladles of several are thin and the ones left behind richer (the last takes whatever remains).
- **Scum** rises as pale blobs while raw meat cooks. **Tap use with the spoon to skim it off.** Any scum left in the pot ends up in your servings as something live, and stirring while it is still there folds it in for good, which skimming cannot fix. So skim first, then stir.
- A pot with no raw meat makes no scum. The pot left alone on a gentle fire is the slow, safe, unskilled way; the spoon buys a harder fire and a cleaner stew.

Stirring a pot hotter than the handle stands burns your hand; see [Spoon](Spoon.md#wear-and-hazards).

A serving is a **Broth** (mostly water), a **Stew**, or a **Scorched Stew**. Eating it gives vanilla hunger and saturation; anything live or toxic left in it gives Hunger or Poison as in raw food.
