# Hand Hammer

A stone hammer you swing yourself, in a crafting grid. It's the first way to make plates, before you have any machines, and it teaches you the mod's main lesson the hard way.

## Crafting

```
S S S
S T S
  T
```

S is Cobblestone and T is a Stick.

## Using it

Put the hammer and **one** workpiece anywhere in a crafting grid. The output slot shows what that piece will be **after one more blow**. Take it, and that's one swing: the hammer loses a little durability and the piece takes one blow's worth of work.

Keep swinging and watch the result change. Each swing lands **3 St**, which is 3 Fu on copper: a copper ingot becomes a **Copper Plate** on the fifth swing, **Copper Foil** on the eighth, then **Copper Scrap**.

> **Careful with shift-click.** Shift-clicking swings until the material runs out, which takes an ingot straight past plate and foil and into scrap. That's the mod's core idea in miniature: whatever is doing the hitting doesn't know when to stop. See [Metalworking](Metalworking.md).

## What it can work

It's a light blow, 3 St. It can shape metals with a hardness of 3 or less: **copper (1), tin (0.6), zinc (2), gold (0.5) and lead (0.3)**. It can also hammer **nuggets into bullets** (see [Ammunition](Ammunition.md)).

It does **nothing** to harder metals like brass (4), bronze (5), iron (6) or steel (15). The craft simply isn't offered. For those you need a [Mechanical Hammer](Mechanical_Hammer.md) with the right gearing.

Very soft metals move a lot with each blow: 3 St on lead (0.3) is 10 Fu a swing, so it's easy to overshoot with them.

## Wear

Unlike machines, the hand hammer wears out from **normal use**, like any hand tool. It's good for **250 swings**, about fifty copper plates, and then it breaks. They're cheap.
