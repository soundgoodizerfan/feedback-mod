# Armor

Helmets, chestplates, leggings and boots are shaped from plate at a bench, and the plate is the piece: it comes out ready to wear.

## Making the piece

Shape the plate at the bench for its stock: the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) (metal, with a [Hand Hammer](Hand_Hammer.md)), the [Woodworking Table](Woodworking_Table.md) (wood, bone, leather, fiber, with an axe) or the [Stonecutter](Stonecutter.md) (stone, ceramic, glass, crystal, no tool). Wooden plates are sawn and shaped at the Woodworking Table. The tool loses a point. Put stock in as many cells as the shape has. What comes out is the finished piece, plain, and it can be worn at once.

Stock is ingots, plates, leather, gems or chain, all the same material. A metal with a hot-working window (steel) has to be hot to shape, as under any hammer.

## Lining and studs

Put the piece in the center of a bench and fit to it a **lining** (anything fibrous: leather, wool) for a little extra protection, and/or **studs** (a pair), one stack of stock at a time from the stock slot or by clicking the empty place in the fan:

| On the piece | Gives |
| --- | --- |
| nothing | plain |
| lining | lined |
| studs | studded |
| lining, studs | lined and studded |

Click a fitted lining or studs in the fan to take them off; the piece stays whole and is worn as it is.

### Studs

**Studs** are gems, shards or nuggets set into the plate (brigandine; diamond studs in steel plate). Each stud covers a tenth of the piece, up to half, and that share takes the stud's hardness, toughness and wear. Four diamond studs in a steel chestplate lift it from steel's 6.7 points to about 7.2 and keep steel's toughness, where a whole diamond plate is 7.5 points and glass. Studs add a little mass and nothing else. Armor takes no tip: a plate has no edge.

## What it gives

- **Protection depends on how hard and strong the plate is.** Iron gives what it does in vanilla: six points in a chestplate. Diamond and netherite give more; **gold and leather give much less**, because they're soft. A plate that is better than iron gives less and less extra: there's a ceiling.
- **Chain covers less than plate** (three quarters as much).
- **Armor toughness** only comes from the very best plate (diamond, netherite).
- **Dense metal resists being pushed.** Heavy metals like gold and netherite give knockback resistance.
- **Weight slows you.** A chestplate of dense metal (gold is nearly three times iron's weight) makes you a little slower. Iron is light enough not to.

The piece is drawn in a plain gray sprite **tinted by the plate**, so you can see at a glance what someone is wearing.

## Wearing out

Armor wears like any [tool](Tools_and_Weapons.md#when-it-breaks-mending-and-swapping-parts): it stops one point short of gone and gives nothing until it's repaired by putting it in the center of a bench and picking stock of the plate's material. Old vanilla armor you find is converted; diamond armor becomes [steel with four diamond studs](Tools_and_Weapons.md#old-vanilla-tools).

Armor can be [plated](Tools_and_Weapons.md#plating). A sound coat of a harder metal is a skin over the whole plate and adds about a tenth of its hardness.

[Guns](Guns.md#hardness-glass-glancing-armor) read the plate's hardness to decide what a bullet goes through.
