# Potion Salts

A potion bottle fades: its strength halves every few days, faster when warm. Salts keep it. A **Vacuum Dryer** takes the water out of a bottle and leaves crystals that carry the potion's effect, level and strength as they were when it was dried. Salts never fade and stack with others of the same kind.

## Vacuum Dryer

A chamber set on a fire, with a vacuum pump on a shaft across it (the same shaft rule as the Gas Compressor). Turning the shaft draws the chamber down, deeper the faster it turns, to a floor of -0.9 Pu; when the shaft stops, air leaks back in.

- Use a potion bottle on it to load it. A dud draught is refused.
- It dries while the chamber is at least **60 Tu** and at or under **-0.5 Pu**, for 200 ticks (10 s). Low pressure is why 60 Tu is enough. If either condition lapses the drying waits where it is.
- An empty hand takes the salts out; with nothing finished it returns the bottle, or reads the temperature, pressure and progress.
- The Pressure Gauge and a thermometer read the chamber.

Running correctly costs nothing and the machine does not wear. Two mistakes cost the potion:

- **Scorching.** Above **110 Tu** the contents scorch: every tick spent hotter takes a little of the potion's strength, more the hotter it is, up to four fifths of it. The loss is fixed in the salts.
- **Cracking.** Above **200 Tu** under vacuum the bottle can crack and is lost.

## Reconstituting

Use the salts on a water source. One portion is spent and a potion bottle comes back with about 70% of the salts' strength, a little more or less each time. Its effect and level are unchanged; as a bottle it fades again. Keeping a potion in salts trades strength for time.
