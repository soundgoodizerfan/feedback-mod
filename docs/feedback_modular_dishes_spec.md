## 1. What was asked

- **Modular dishes (ruled).** A dish is assembled from parts the player made, not matched to a recipe or poured from a pot.
- A dish is **a tool type**, as a gun is (modular-items spec 8.3): a `ModularDesign` of parts with materials and discrete choices, baked once, with the core's traits, adjectives, Shift glosses, tooltips, JEI cards and a table that is the Tool Table's code with a type filter. No second implementation.

## 2. The model

A **design** is a layout plus ordered parts; a part is a **role**, a **material** (here: a food material from the food spec, `material/*.json` with a `food` block) and a few **discrete choices**. What differs from a pick or a sword is that every part carries its own **`FoodState`** (class on the ladder, moisture, pathogen, toxin, rot) from the processes that made it, so the bake reads state, not just material.

**Baking** stays a pure function, now of `(design, material table, each part's state at assembly)`. It runs once at assembly and stores the figures on the stack as a component. The finished dish then has its own `FoodState` and clock (food spec 4.1 and 4.2) from that moment, and eating it is food spec 4.5.

### 2.1 Roles

| Role | What it is | Where it comes from |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **container** | holds the dish and sets what may be wet | a bowl (ceramic), a plate, a flatbread or wrap, a pastry shell, a skewer |
| **base** | the bulk | grain, bread, a pot serving of broth or stew, dough |
| **main** | the dense part | cooked meat, legume, fish, egg |
| **side** | texture, fibre, bulk | vegetables, fruit, a pickle |
| **finish** | a small, strong addition, added last at the Prepare tab (`after` role, not an assembly slot) | sauce, seasoning, honey, salt, herbs |

A type uses a subset (a soup is container, base, finish -- built; a wrap is container, main, side, finish). Always at most five parts, so the Tool Table's six slots hold any dish. Roles are filled by **properties** (`Role.accepts`), never by item id, exactly as in modular-items §4.2.

The **container** is the shaped part, an explicit item of its own (the rule from modular-items §4.2: an ambiguous item must never decide the type). A bowl is ceramic or wood from the Parts mode; a flatbread or pastry shell is baked dough from the food spec's ladder (flatbread built 2026-10-24: a food container, `wrapper` trait; pastry shell not built). The other roles take food items directly. **Food shapes** (2026-10-24, user request): a type's optional `shapes` list names foods its bench lays from stock in the Parts tab beside the containers, each a `Shaping` with stock `traits` (`kneadable`): flatbread dough (wrap) and noodles (bowl, three dough in a row, a `starchy` base). The output is the food item itself, not a `ModularPartItem`, one per piece, copied from the stock so its state carries; it fills a role as any food does. `[OPEN]` meat shapes (a patty, a meatball): a shape that keeps which animal it came from needs a part-like `MadeOf` on a food, and the food roles find a material by item.

### 2.2 Discrete choices (per part)

- **Cut:** whole, sliced, minced. Chosen at the table with a knife. A smaller piece has less mass to heat through and more surface: it cooks and dries faster (food spec 3), carries compounds faster and spoils faster. It also eats faster (§2.4). A real trade, not a default.
- **Container fill (built, ruled):** the food parts' volumes (`DISH_PART_MB` each) against the container's volume (role litres x cells). Over capacity builds (legal) and **spills**: each time the dish is carried (picked up or thrown down; `FoodEvents`) a share (`SPILL_SHARE`) of the overflow is lost from the **wettest** part's hunger and saturation, down to what the container holds; pathogen and toxin stay. Wear from a specific mistake, never from uptime.
- **Seal (built, ruled: a container property):** the container role's `seal`: 0 open, 1 covered (a wrap folds over its contents), 2 sealed (a lid, a jar). Covered halves drying, migration and spill; sealed stops them and stops rot. Not a per-dish choice. Sealed is reserved for modded containers: `Canning`'s sealed is a flag on a jar item, not reusable here, and no item makes a sealed dish.

No sliders. A dish with five parts has a few dozen legal builds, not a continuous design space.

### 2.3 What the bake derives

| Figure | From |
| :--- | :--- |
| hunger, saturation | each part's authored figure scaled by its state (food spec 4.5), summed, **capped by the container's volume**: a bowl cannot hold a feast, so a bigger plate is a bigger meal |
| moisture | **built:** mass-weighted blend (unchanged by what follows), then **migration**: wet parts wet the dry ones (`MIGRATION`, scaled by the container's seal): a dry part beside a soggy one is soaked, its doneness drops a step (dried to done) and it feeds `SOAK_SCALE` of its figures. Soaking-through is both a moisture raise and, past the limit, a scale; spilling is the other loss (2.2) |
| pathogen | blended; **any raw pathogen part taints the whole** (cross-contamination), and a heat step on the assembled dish (an oven) can cure it |
| toxin | a concentration; the blend dilutes it and the container's volume sets the water in it (food spec 4.4) |
| keeping time | **built:** the dish's own `FoodState` (rot of the worst part, blended water) on the lazy clock: wetter and warmer rots faster, cold and dry keep it |
| compounds | summed per compound across parts, by concentration, then released by fat and fibre (food spec 7) |
| eating time | from the mass and cut (§2.4) |
| heat rating | the container's `service_temperature`: a hot dish in a thin container burns the hand (`HotContact`) |

Nothing here is authored per dish, so it cannot be balanced into one right answer (modular-items §2.1's depth argument).

### 2.4 Why no dish is best

The no-best-food check (food spec ruling 5) applied to this type:

- Hunger is capped by the container, and **a bigger container is slower to eat** (eating time scales with mass, so a feast is a risk in a fight).
- A strong compound is a *concentration*: pile the same sugar in two parts and the lift is bigger and the crash after is worse.
- A wet dish lifts saturation (carried by fat and fibre) but spoils fast and spills; a dry dish keeps for a trip but starts its buff late.
- Raw, fresh parts give the most per item but bring their pathogen; cooking each part first is slower and safer. Hand work and automation both lead here, at different costs.
- A finish is small by mass, so it moves the compounds more than the hunger: great for a buff, no use for a meal.

No variety bonus, no flavour synergy table, no "perfect dish" score. Those are identity-keyed or a free scalar to grind (Cuisine Delight, ModularDelight both do them; rejected).

## 3. The table

A **Prep Table**: the Tool Table with the dish type filter (modular-items §4.2, as the Gun Table is). It has the same six slots, the same inference (the parts imply the dish; no type picker), the same audit (two types may not accept one part set), and a **knife slot** where the Tool Table has a hammer. Every cut spends a point of the knife, the hand tool's accepted wear. `[OPEN]` whether the knife is any `HandKnife`-class item, a sword part, or a new hand tool; and whether a Hearth-like heat (a pan beside the table) is wanted for hot assembly. Assembly of a cold dish needs no heat.

**Taking a dish apart (built, ruled)** returns its parts with their states (a dish that has sat is not the fresh parts it was made of); the container returns whole. A finished dish in the work slot and the **Take apart** button on the Prepare tab (a point of the knife, as a cut): the server re-reads the slot, settles the dish's clock and rebuilds each food as the item of its material's form with a `FoodState` from the part's dims (cook, moisture, pathogen, toxin, charred, smoke, salt; rot the worse of the part's and the dish's, the dish's stamp and envTu so spoilage goes on; moisture scaled by the drying since baking) and its `FOOD_CUT`; a shaped container (bowl, plate, skewer) returns whole as its part (`ToolDesigns.items`), a flatbread as the food it is; the finish and any salt are lost (they are on the dish); a part whose material has no item returns nothing. A **soup cannot be taken apart** (a stew is a mixture): refused with a line saying so, nothing spent. Parts go to the inventory by `placeItemBackInInventory`, as the Tool Table's dismantle does, and what does not fit drops at the player.

## 4. Minigames (ruled: wanted)

Rhythm is forging's, drag is soldering's; the pot and skillet are in the food spec. Two for dishes, both **placement** and **flow** games with analog input, server-recomputed from the submitted samples:

- **Cut: place the marks.** A part is shown; the player clicks to set cut lines. Even spacing gives even pieces (even cooking and drying later); uneven leaves a thick and a thin piece, which the later heat step cooks apart. Grades are classed (even, mixed, ragged); a ragged cut is bad, never refused.
- **Finish: pour or sprinkle.** Happens at the end, on the real dish: assemble it first, then hold to pour a sauce or sprinkle a seasoning onto the finished dish (drawn on screen, the sauce coating it); release at the marks. One finish per dish. Flow follows the finish's viscosity (existing `molten.viscosity` or a food equivalent): honey is slow and precise, broth is fast and floods. Overshoot concentrates the compound (a stronger lift, a worse crash), so overpouring is a choice with a price, not only a failure.

**The reveal is a prediction (ruled).** The cut, pour and knead screens run the same pure function as the server on the player's own input, so the grade word and the music can play at once; the server then confirms and applies from the submitted samples. The client never decides what is stored. If the server refuses a finish (no session, wrong item, too fast), it sends the HUD line "The work did not take." (`prep.feedback.refused`) so the prediction is not left standing.

Automation builds the same dishes with no minigame at the lowest class (even if sloppy, never a refusal), so a dish is possible without skill and better with it. Hand skill buys evenness and a clean pour, never a part.

## 5. Reading what you made

Same as modular-items §6. The dish shows adjectives from its figures (soggy, rich, dense, bright, risky) with Shift glosses from the constants that decide them, and **names itself from structure**: container class, the dominant role's classes, and the finish's. `"Dried-Beef Barley Bowl"`, `"Sliced-Egg Wrap"`. It never matches an item id (ModularDelight's names did). Figures read through `Readout.figure` at the best carried instrument's tier, exact at `Instrument.TRUTH`. New `Quantity` entries for moisture, pathogen load and toxin concentration; the zero-tier instrument is a **bite** (nibbling reveals the hazard class at the cost of a small dose). **Built 2026-10-24** (see the status line): the strip yes, a core-temperature thermometer no.

A **blueprint** is optional: a drawn dish predicts figures if every part comes out to drawing, as for tools. `[OPEN]` whether dishes get blueprints at all; a recipe the player can keep is useful, but it risks becoming the recipe book the design avoids. Default: no, until the tool path exists and play asks for it.

## Kept on purpose: perks on a dish

Material perks (`Material.perks`) on a dish's parts apply to a target the dish strikes (`DishItem.postHurtEnemy` goes through `MaterialPerks`). A skewer with honey slows what it hits, because honey carries `cling`. Found by accident in play (2026-10-24) and kept (author ruling). Not a bug: do not strip perks from dishes. Worth remembering when authoring food materials that a perk is a property that will fire on a swing.


## The bite gesture (author ruling, 2026-10-24)

Sneak and TAP right-click nibbles; sneak and HOLD eats as usual. Built as: a sneak-press on a food that can be eaten starts vanilla eating; a release within `FTuning.BITE_TAP_TICKS` of starting (`LivingEntityUseItemEvent.Stop`) is the nibble. A player too full to eat has no eating to start, so the press itself nibbles. This replaces the first build, which cancelled every sneak-click on food and so stopped a sneaking player from eating.
